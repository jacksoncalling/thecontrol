## Prompt (pasted exactly, from prompts/q05.txt)

```
Context: The Larder team (invented meal-planning app) is considering a solution: "Scan your supermarket receipt and Larder proposes next week's dinners from what you already bought." They wrote down these assumptions:

(a) Households want Larder to propose a week of dinners based on what they already bought.
(b) Users will find and understand the "scan receipt" button on the home screen.
(c) Our text recognition can read receipts from the three largest supermarket chains with at least 95% accuracy.
(d) Enough free users will upgrade to the paid tier to cover the cost of the receipt-reading service.
(e) Storing receipts will not expose sensitive purchases (for example medication or alcohol) in ways that could harm users.
(f) Users will bother to scan a receipt after most shopping trips.

Using Teresa Torres' five assumption types (desirability, viability, feasibility, usability, ethical), label each assumption with one type and give a one-line reason.
```

## Response (raw, copied with the copy button, not edited)

Here are the labels, using Teresa Torres' five assumption types:

- **(a) Desirability:** It tests whether households actually want a week of dinners proposed from what they already bought, which is a question about customer need and value.
- **(b) Usability:** It tests whether users can find and understand the "scan receipt" button, which is about discoverability and comprehension of the interface.
- **(c) Feasibility:** It tests whether the text recognition technology can reach 95% accuracy on the three largest chains' receipts, which is a question of what we can build.
- **(d) Viability:** It tests whether enough free users convert to paid to cover the receipt-reading cost, which is about the business model working for Larder.
- **(e) Ethical:** It tests whether stored receipts could expose sensitive purchases like medication or alcohol and harm users, which is about unintended consequences.
- **(f) Desirability:** It tests whether users will bother to scan a receipt after most shopping trips, which is about whether the value is worth the effort to them.

A note on (f): it sits close to usability, since it's about effort. I labeled it desirability because the question is whether users care enough to do it repeatedly, not whether they can figure out how. If the team's concern were instead that scanning is clumsy or slow, usability would be the better fit.
