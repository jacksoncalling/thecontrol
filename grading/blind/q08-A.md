# Q08 answer A

In Teresa Torres' framework, an opportunity is a customer need, pain point, or desire, stated from the customer's perspective and not tied to a solution. It sits between the desired outcome (a measurable business or product outcome) and the solutions.

**(a) "I need a button that sorts my list by aisle."** No, this is a solution. It belongs at the solution level, probably under (b).

**(b) "I waste time walking back and forth in the store."** Yes. It's a customer pain point, and it doesn't name a solution.

**(c) "I want a weekly meal-kit delivery."** No, this is a solution. Ask what need sits behind it (less planning effort, less shopping time) and put that at the opportunity level.

**(d) "I don't know who will be home for dinner on which nights."** Yes. It's a customer need in the customer's own words, with several possible solutions.

**(e) "Increase the share of households who cook at least three planned dinners per week."** No, this is an outcome. It belongs at the top of the tree as the desired outcome, and it's a good one because it's a product-usage behavior the team can influence.

**(f) "We need more free users to upgrade to premium."** No, this is a business outcome, and it isn't a customer need. It would sit at the outcome level, but it's a lagging business metric. Torres advises a product outcome (a customer behavior the team can drive) as the team's outcome, with this as the downstream result.

**(g) "I want Larder to sync with my partner's calendar."** No, this is a solution. It's a possible answer to (d), so move it under that opportunity.

**(h) "I throw away food I forgot I had bought."** Yes. It's a customer pain point with no solution baked in.

**Summary:** (b), (d) and (h) belong at the opportunity level. (a), (c) and (g) are solutions. (e) and (f) are outcomes, and (e) is the better candidate for the team's desired outcome.
