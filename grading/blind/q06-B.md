# Q06 answer B

Test **A2 (feasibility)** first.

**The reasoning tool:** Teresa Torres uses **assumption mapping**: a 2x2 that plots each assumption by how much evidence you have (x-axis: more evidence ↔ less evidence) and how important it is (y-axis). Importance is judged by the "if this were false" question: would the idea fail, or could you work around it? You start with assumptions in the quadrant that is **important and has little or no evidence**, because they carry the most risk and you know the least about them. Assumptions that are important but already well supported (A1) need no testing yet. Assumptions that are unimportant (A3, A4) can wait, whatever their evidence.

**Placing the four:**

|Assumption|Importance (idea fails if false?)|Evidence|Quadrant|
|---|---|---|---|
|A1 desirability|High|Strong (9 of 12 specific recent stories)|Important, well supported → no test needed now|
|A2 feasibility|High (idea fails)|None|**Important, no evidence → test first**|
|A3 usability|Low (default sorting is a fix)|None|Unimportant → defer|
|A4 viability|Low-moderate (idea could still proceed)|Some (€40 vs €200 limit, a 5x margin)|Unimportant, supported → defer|

**Why A2:** It is the only assumption where failure kills the idea and the team has zero evidence. Nobody even knows a data source exists. A1 is important, but the interview evidence is specific and behavioral (real stories, not opinions), so it is already reasonably de-risked. A3 and A4 would not sink the idea even if wrong.

**A cheap way to test it:** Torres favors fast tests, not builds. For example, within a week or two, pick the 3 to 5 supermarket chains your users shop at most, and for each one check whether layout data is obtainable (a public API, a data partner, crowdsourcing, a store-staff contact, a licensing conversation). Also test whether the data holds at the store level, since layouts differ between branches of the same chain. Set a clear pass/fail bar upfront, such as "we can get usable layouts for stores covering at least X% of our users." If A2 fails, you have saved the team from building a feature on data that doesn't exist.
