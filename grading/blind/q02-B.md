# Q02 answer B

|#|Group|Why|
|---|---|---|
|1|A|Specific occasion: last Sunday at four, at the kitchen table.|
|2|A|Recalled event: she started with the fridge after throwing out spinach and chicken the week before.|
|3|A|Concrete action and result: she typed "spinach" and got about forty results.|
|4|A|The event is texting her partner. The "Tuesdays and Thursdays" clause is a generalisation, but the statement is mainly the specific act.|
|5|A|He answered two hours later, after she had finished the list.|
|6|A|She had to redo Thursday.|
|7|A|Monday in the shop, the list was alphabetical, and it took almost an hour.|
|8|D|Feature request (sort by aisle).|
|9|B|"Usually... everybody does" is a generalisation about her own habit and other people.|
|10|D|Wish or feature request (calendar sync).|
|11|C|Hypothetical future behaviour ("I'd probably pay").|
|12|D|An opinion or assessment ("the real problem"), not an event. It is a good lead to probe with "tell me about the last time leftovers were a problem."|

Torres treats group A, specific stories about past behaviour, as the most reliable evidence. People are poor at predicting what they would do (C), and generalisations and opinions (B, D) blend several occasions into a smooth composite. A single recalled event keeps the real sequence, friction and context. In this excerpt those details point to unmet needs such as starting from what's already in the fridge, confirming who is home before planning, and a list ordered for the shop. Statements 8 and 10 are solution signals, and 11 is not evidence of willingness to pay.
