## Prompt (pasted exactly, from prompts/q08.txt)

```
Context: The Larder team (invented meal-planning app for households) is building an opportunity solution tree. Team members proposed these cards for the opportunity level:

(a) "I need a button that sorts my list by aisle."
(b) "I waste time walking back and forth in the store."
(c) "I want a weekly meal-kit delivery."
(d) "I don't know who will be home for dinner on which nights."
(e) "Increase the share of households who cook at least three planned dinners per week."
(f) "We need more free users to upgrade to premium."
(g) "I want Larder to sync with my partner's calendar."
(h) "I throw away food I forgot I had bought."

Using Teresa Torres' definitions, say for each card whether it belongs at the opportunity level. If it does not, say where it belongs or what is wrong with it, in one line.
```

## Response (raw, copied with the copy button, not edited)

Four of the eight belong at the opportunity level: (b), (d), (h), and arguably none of the others. The test I used is Torres' question: _is there more than one way to address this?_ If not, it's a solution.

|Card|Opportunity?|Where it belongs / what's wrong|
|---|---|---|
|(a) Sort-by-aisle button|No|Solution. It's one specific feature. The need behind it is (b), so put it under (b).|
|(b) Walking back and forth in the store|**Yes**|Pain point, in the customer's voice, with many possible solutions.|
|(c) Weekly meal-kit delivery|No|Solution (a feature request). The need behind it isn't stated, maybe "I don't have time to plan or shop" or "I don't know what to cook." Tag it `[NEEDS FOLLOW-UP]` and ask in the next interview what problem the kit would solve.|
|(d) Don't know who's home for dinner on which nights|**Yes**|Need or pain point, stated from the customer's perspective, with many ways to address it.|
|(e) Share of households cooking 3+ planned dinners/week|No|This is the product outcome, a customer behavior in the product, with a measurable direction. It's the root node of the tree, not a node under it.|
|(f) More free users upgrading to premium|No|Business outcome (a revenue lever). It's not customer-facing, and the team can't control it through product decisions alone. Ask which customer behavior would drive upgrades and use that as the outcome. It also can't share a tree with (e), because one tree holds one outcome.|
|(g) Sync with partner's calendar|No|Solution. It's one way to address (d), so put it under (d).|
|(h) Throwing away food I forgot I bought|**Yes**|Pain point with many possible solutions.|

Two things to check when you build the tree:

- (b), (d), and (h) are distinct siblings, so none of them collapses into another.
- Each one is only as strong as the interview behind it. If these came from team brainstorming rather than story-based interviews, mark them as provisional until customers confirm them with specific recent stories.
