## Prompt (pasted exactly, from prompts/q05.txt)

```
Context: The Larder team (invented meal-planning app) is considering a solution: "Scan your supermarket receipt and Larder proposes next week's dinners from what you already bought." They wrote down these assumptions:

(a) Households want Larder to propose a week of dinners based on what they already bought.
(b) Users will find and understand the "scan receipt" button on the home screen.
(c) Our text recognition can read receipts from the three largest supermarket chains with at least 95% accuracy.
(d) Enough free users will upgrade to the paid tier to cover the cost of the receipt-reading service.
(e) Storing receipts will not expose sensitive purchases (for example medication or alcohol) in ways that could harm users.
(f) Users will bother to scan a receipt after most shopping trips.

Using Teresa Torres' five assumption types (desirability, viability, feasibility, usability, ethical), label each assumption with one type and give a one-line reason.
```

## Response (raw, copied with the copy button, not edited)

||Assumption|Type|Reason|
|---|---|---|---|
|a|Households want a week of dinners proposed from what they bought|**Desirability**|It asks whether customers want the outcome at all.|
|b|Users will find and understand the "scan receipt" button|**Usability**|They may want the feature but fail to discover or use it.|
|c|Text recognition reads the three biggest chains' receipts at 95%+|**Feasibility**|It asks whether we can build it, which depends on technical accuracy.|
|d|Enough free users upgrade to cover the receipt-reading cost|**Viability**|It asks whether the business model sustains the feature.|
|e|Stored receipts won't expose sensitive purchases in harmful ways|**Ethical**|It asks whether we should build it, given the risk of harm to users.|
|f|Users will scan a receipt after most shopping trips|**Desirability**|It asks whether people will repeat the behavior, which shows how much they want the value.|

Assumption (f) could also be read as usability, since scanning takes effort. I labeled it desirability because the question is whether the payoff is worth that effort. If your team's worry is that scanning is clumsy rather than unrewarding, move it to usability.

Two of the six are desirability, which Torres suggests testing first in most cases. But (c) and (e) could kill the solution outright and can be checked in hours (a sample-receipt accuracy test, a privacy review), so they are worth running early too.

I can map these to the riskiest-first order and draft the smallest test for each, if you tell me the outcome this solution serves and which two competing solutions it's being compared against.
